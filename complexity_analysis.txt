COMPLEXITY ANALYSIS

Given:
V = 6 vertices
E = 6 undirected edges

1. Adjacency Matrix
- Space: O(V^2)
- BFS: O(V^2)
- DFS: O(V^2)
- Edge checking: O(1)
- Searching a vertex by graph traversal: O(V^2) worst case when BFS/DFS is used.

2. Adjacency List
- Space: O(V + E)
- BFS: O(V + E)
- DFS: O(V + E)
- Edge checking: O(degree(u)) for a vertex u; O(V) worst case.
- Searching a vertex by graph traversal: O(V + E) worst case.

Execution for target F:
- Both representations examine A, B and C before discovering F.
- Matrix: 18 matrix-position checks (3 processed vertices × 6 positions).
- List: 7 neighbour checks (A: 2, B: 3, C: 2).

For edge E-F:
- Matrix: 1 direct lookup.
- List: 2 neighbour checks because E's list is B, F.

The graph has 6 edges among 6 vertices and is sparse relative to the
maximum possible number of undirected edges, V(V-1)/2 = 15.
Therefore, the adjacency list uses less storage and avoids scanning
non-existent edges during traversal.
